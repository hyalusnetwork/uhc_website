# UHC Season Archive

About is an illustrated field guide based on `references/about-user.md`, with the supplied End Portal photograph in `dist/images/end-portal.webp`. Its markup is preserved in `about-page.html`; `revamp-about.cjs` regenerates it and updates the About section in `dist/index.html`. Styling lives in `dist/about.css`. `make-html.cjs` embeds the photograph, item icons, and About styles into `../UHC.html` for offline use. `qa-about-revamp.cjs` checks section navigation, Crown entry, image loading, runtime errors, and widths from 320 to 1440 pixels against headless Edge on port 9232.

Open `dist/index.html` in a browser. This is a buildless website and also works from a static web host.

The import contains 36 seasons and 515 participant records from `UHC_Data.xlsx`. The workbook is unchanged. To refresh, run `./import-workbook.ps1` and then `node prepare-data.cjs` from this directory. The importer reads the workbook in the parent folder and `prepare-data.cjs` generates `dist/data.js`.

Seasons include player and team search, year filtering, chronological sorting, a season index, Ironman badges, debut badges, and crown tooltips. Players adds full profiles for players with at least five recorded games, combining only aliases explicitly listed in the bios workbook. Search accepts current and former names. Profiles include trophy cases, season histories, teams, frequent teammates/victims/killers, kill and survival charts, generated trivia, and expandable historical descriptions. Trivia and Links remain reserved tabs.

Stats recreates all 12 table categories and all 6 graph categories found on https://hyalus.shinyapps.io/hyalus_uhc/. Its tables support search, column sorting, player/season links and CSV export. Graphs have formatted hover, focus and tap tooltips. Totals are recalculated from the workbook for all recorded players, including those below the profile threshold. Histogram bins are five minutes, outcome percentages exclude survivors, and the timeline measures days since the previous season, using workbook dates. The timeline uses an axis break when an empty range between short and long gaps can be omitted. Historical grade coloring is no longer used.

The original site's 110 PNG assets are saved in `dist/faces/`. `face-manifest.json` preserves download provenance and name mappings. Recovered faces appear in season rows, roster strips, player cards, profiles and statistics. Unmatched players use Steve. `make-html.cjs` embeds the PNG data for offline use; refreshing the workbook never redownloads assets.

From the parent Website folder, run `./Refresh-UHC.ps1` after updating either workbook, then reload `UHC.html`. All statistics regenerate from the ledger. The page embeds its PNGs, styles, scripts, and data; it makes no network requests and uses no hosting.

Rankings compares recorded team colors, with a sticky color index, color/team/player/season search, eligibility filters, sortable standings and one appearance table per color. Ranking requires more than five appearances (at least six). Five-appearance colors and special groupings remain browsable without ranks. Participant entries count repeat participation across seasons; color appearances count each season once.

The 0–100 performance score scales adjusted win rate against a 40% benchmark (60 points), adjusted kills per participant against 0.75 (25 points), and adjusted survivor rate against 40% (15 points). Each component caps at its weight. These fixed strong-performance benchmarks broaden the score range without forcing the best current color to receive 100; 100 means meeting all benchmarks rather than perfect play. Win rates add five prior appearances at the overall color average; combat/survival rates add ten prior participant entries at the respective averages. This dampens small samples. Raw wins/appearances, kills/participants and survivors/participants stay visible beside the score. The formula and contributions are explained on the page. The score is a proposed comparison method and can be revised without changing the recorded histories.

The native Hardcore-heart PNG is embedded as the favicon and navbar/footer mark, in addition to the hero graphic.

Trivia includes 38 current record categories and 41 trivia entries, informed by the original site's Records tab, the first `UHC Records` worksheet of `Hyalus UHC Trivia & Records.xlsx`, and the `UHC Trivia` section of `Hyalus UHC Trivia & Charts.docx`. It uses a compact, mixed record and trivia feed with diamond-cyan all-time records and emerald-green single-season records, topic/content filters, player/season search, record-rule tooltips, linked players/seasons and expandable instance lists. Nine gameplay stories that cannot be established from the ledger are explicitly marked as historical document notes. The two final deaths outside the Overworld are marked as user-confirmed trivia. Original record tables remain available inside the Sources section with historical totals kept separate from current cards. The attached files are preserved in `references/`, with extracted snapshots alongside the source code.

`trivia-engine.cjs` regenerates ledger-based records, feats and death milestones during normal workbook refresh. It uses all combined player identities, including players below the profile threshold. Timed death milestones exclude disconnects and disqualifications. Ties share records, and match lengths use the last recorded elimination. Winning streaks and transitions use consecutive games played; the Ironman streak record uses consecutive scheduled seasons. Historical source wording is preserved with the requested current-name replacement for Happy_Guy555.

Rankings has two full-width mode buttons: Player Rankings and Team Color Rankings. The existing color view is retained inside its own panel, with unchanged scoring and tables. `#rankings` opens team colors; `#rankings-players` opens players; `#rank-player-<encoded name>` opens a player's score breakdown. Player Rankings includes a top-three showcase, searchable/sortable leaderboard, overall and individual-stat S/A/B/C/D/F tiers, keyboard/hover/tap tooltips, score contribution bars, recent-five-game links and profile links.

Player ranking requires five games. The 0–100 formula awards up to 40 points for kills per game (benchmark 1.5), 30 for win rate (40%), 20 for Defense and 10 for survival (50%). Defense splits into 10 points for Ironman rate (30% benchmark) and 10 for durability (80% benchmark). Durability is the equal-game average share of other timed PVP/PVE/Team Kill deaths outlasted. Own deaths are excluded; tied times are not outlasted; survivors outlast all timed deaths. Games without another timed death or with unknown exit times are excluded with coverage shown. Positive rates add five prior games at the participant-weighted all-player averages. Team Kills deduct 40 times Team Kills per game, capped at 20 points. Overall and positive-stat tiers use thresholds 80/65/50/35/20; stat tiers are percentages of their adjusted benchmark. Discipline is S for zero Team Kills, otherwise A/B/C/D/F at rates ≤0.02/0.05/0.10/0.20/>0.20 per game. Zero Team Kills does not award free score points. The page explains the formula and makes the raw rates visible. Ranking data regenerates with the workbook.

Wins count all members of the winning team, including dead teammates. A winning team is inferred when it alone has players without a recorded death or exit, with explicitly documented historical victories as a fallback. PVP kills exclude team kills. First Blood and Final Boss use PVP death times; ties share awards. Team Wipe requires every recorded member of an opposing team of at least two players to be killed by the same player. Special achievements such as Perfect Ironman and Overpowered use explicit records only. Repeated Overpowered rows count as recorded apple events. Death milestones count PVP, PVE, and team-kill deaths in chronological ledger order; disconnects and disqualifications do not count. These definitions also appear in the page.

The historical descriptions and trivia are preserved verbatim, safely escaped, and clearly labeled as historical. They are not used as current rankings or totals. The engine recomputes current milestones; for example, its 150th-death record differs from the historical Enzo_ bio.

Debuts use the first recorded season of each exact player name; aliases are not combined. Ironman and crown methods come from explicit workbook records. A crown retained by a nonparticipant appears in the season footer. Blank death records remain N/A. The duration shown is the last recorded elimination, since the workbook has no separate match-end field. Minecraft version text is preserved as stored. Uploaders and recording URLs are absent from the workbook.

Team joins are imported from sheet 5 (`Season`, `Player`, `Team Joined`, `Time`). Simultaneous entries for the same team are grouped into one event and merged chronologically into Seasons tables. Season metadata is read by header name, including `Roamer(s)` and `Version`; the inserted roamer column does not shift version values. Roamer badges identify players with recorded joins in non-Find Your Team roamer games. A missing joining timestamp is not inferred. Future runs of Refresh-UHC.ps1 import all five sheets and rebuild these events.

## Preserved material from the original site

Downloaded site pages, source code, browser profiles, and one-time extraction scripts have been removed. The current site keeps its local images in dist/faces/ and dist/icons/, face-manifest.json for image mappings and provenance, and trivia-legacy-records.json for carried-over historical records. The document and workbook reference files remain available for rebuilding historical text. stats-category-reference.json preserves only the original category names used by the statistics checks. Normal refreshes use these local assets and do not require the old website.
